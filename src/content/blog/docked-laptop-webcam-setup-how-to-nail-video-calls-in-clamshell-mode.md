## Why a Separate Webcam Matters in Clamshell Mode

A laptop’s built‑in camera is hidden when the lid is closed, so you lose the only eye‑level view you had. Relying on the screen’s webcam (if it even exists) forces you to prop the laptop open, which defeats the purpose of a clean, ergonomic docked setup. An external webcam solves three common pain points:

1. **Eye‑level framing** – Keeps your face centered and reduces the “looking up at a screen” vibe.
2. **Better audio** – The C960’s dual omnidirectional microphones pick up voices up to about 2.4 m away, which is far clearer than most laptop mics.
3. **Consistent lighting** – You can pair the webcam with a dedicated desk lamp for even illumination, regardless of the laptop’s position.

## Step‑by‑Step Docked Laptop Webcam Setup

### 1. Connect the Webcam to Your Dock

Most docking stations expose a handful of USB‑A or USB‑C ports. Plug the C960’s USB cable into any available port on the dock. Because it’s plug‑and‑play, the operating system should automatically recognize it as a video device and a microphone.

> **Tip:** If you’re using a USB‑C hub with Power Delivery (PD), make sure the hub’s data lanes are active. Some cheap hubs prioritize charging and may limit video bandwidth. See our guide on [What to Know About USB‑C Hubs with PD Charging for Desk Setups](/blog/what-to-know-about-usb-c-hubs-with-pd-charging-for-desk-setups/) for details.

### 2. Mount the Webcam

The C960 has a standard ¼‑inch thread, so you can attach it to:

* **Monitor top‑mount** – Many monitor arms have a built‑in ¼‑inch screw. Position the camera at eye level, slightly tilted downwards for a natural angle.
* **Adjustable laptop stand** – If you’re using a stand that raises the laptop screen, the webcam can sit on the top of the stand’s platform.
* **Dedicated clip or tripod** – For a more flexible layout, a small clip‑on mount works well on the edge of a desk.

Make sure the camera isn’t blocked by the monitor bezel; a clear line of sight is essential for the 90° field of view.

### 3. Adjust Software Settings

Open your video‑conference app’s camera settings and select “EMEET C960” as the video source. Most platforms let you preview the feed—use this to fine‑tune the framing:

* **Zoom** – Keep the camera at its default focal length; the fixed range is already optimized for typical desk distances.
* **Exposure & White Balance** – Turn on “auto” unless you have a dedicated lighting setup (see next step).

If you need to use the microphone separately, verify that the app is pulling audio from the C960’s built‑in mics. You can test this in the OS sound settings.

### 4. Light Your Face Properly

Even the best webcam looks flat under harsh overhead lighting. Pair the C960 with a gooseneck LED desk lamp that offers adjustable color temperature. Position the lamp at a 45‑degree angle from your face, just above eye level. Our article on [How to Light Your Zoom Calls with a Gooseneck LED Desk Lamp](/blog/how-to-light-your-zoom-calls-with-a-gooseneck-led-desk-lamp/) walks through the exact lamp placement and settings.

### 5. Tame the Cables

A tidy desk not only looks professional but also prevents accidental unplugging. Use reusable cable ties or Velcro straps to bundle the webcam’s USB cable with the other dock cables. For a clean look, route the cable through a cable‑management channel on the back of your desk or through a desk grommet.

If you run into connectivity hiccups, our [USB Hub Troubleshooting](/blog/usb-hub-troubleshooting-fix-connectivity-speed-and-power-issues-in-your-home-office/) guide can help you diagnose power or bandwidth issues.

### 6. Test Before the Call

Run a quick test call with a colleague or use the built‑in camera preview in your meeting software. Check:

* **Video clarity** – 1080p should be sharp; if it looks pixelated, verify the app isn’t forcing a lower resolution.
* **Audio level** – Speak from your normal desk position; the microphones should capture you clearly without background echo.
* **Privacy cover** – Slide it open only when you’re on a call; close it afterward for peace of mind.

## Optional Enhancements

* **External microphone** – If you often host large meetings, a dedicated tabletop mic can supplement the C960’s built‑in mics.
* **Monitor arm with built‑in cable routing** – Keeps the webcam cable hidden while giving you precise height control.
* **Software filters** – Some apps let you add a subtle background blur; this can be useful if your office space is cluttered.

## Potential Drawbacks

* **Fixed focal range** – The C960’s focus is set between roughly 12 in and 10 ft. If you need to move the camera much closer than 12 in (e.g., mounting it on a very low stand), the image may become soft.
* **No adjustable field of view** – The 90‑degree view is static. If you prefer a narrower frame for a tighter headshot, you’ll need to reposition the camera rather than zoom in digitally.

For anyone running a laptop in clamshell mode on a dock, the EMEET C960 offers a plug‑and‑play solution that delivers crisp 1080p video, reliable omnidirectional audio, and a privacy cover—all without the hassle of driver installations.
