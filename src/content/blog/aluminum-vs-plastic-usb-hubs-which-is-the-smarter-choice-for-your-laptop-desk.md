### Why material matters for a desk‑side hub

When your laptop is the centerpiece of a home office, every peripheral you add should complement its performance and aesthetics. The hub’s enclosure material plays a surprisingly big role in three areas:

1. **Heat management** – Aluminum conducts heat away from the internal circuitry far more efficiently than plastic. This keeps the hub cool during long data transfers, which in turn helps the laptop stay within its optimal temperature range.  
2. **Durability** – A one‑piece aluminum unibody can survive the occasional bump or the weight of a heavy cable bundle better than a thin plastic shell that may crack over time.  
3. **Desk aesthetics** – Aluminum has a sleek, professional appearance that matches most modern laptop stands and keyboards. A plastic hub can look cheap and may clash with a minimalist workspace.

If you already have a tidy desk with a metal laptop stand, an aluminum hub blends in without looking out of place.

### Comparing aluminum and plastic hubs

| Aspect | Aluminum Hub | Plastic Hub |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| **Heat dissipation** | Conducts heat quickly, stays cooler under load | Tends to retain heat, can become warm to the touch |
| **Build quality** | Rigid, feels solid; less likely to warp | Can feel flimsy; may develop cracks after repeated plugging |
| **Weight** | Slightly heavier, which can add stability | Lightweight, but may wobble when cables pull |
| **Design flexibility** | Often features angled or ergonomic designs (e.g., 32‑degree angle) | Usually flat, limited ergonomic shaping |
| **Cost** | Typically a bit pricier | Usually cheaper |

For a laptop that already handles heat with its own cooling system, you might think the hub’s material is a minor detail. In practice, an aluminum hub reduces the risk of thermal throttling on the hub itself, which can indirectly keep your laptop’s USB ports from overheating during sustained transfers.

### Key factors to consider when choosing a hub for your laptop

1. **Port variety** – A good hub should give you the mix of USB‑C and USB‑A you need. The intpw hub offers one USB‑C 3.2 port, two USB‑A 3.2 ports, four USB‑A 3.0 ports, and two USB‑C PD ports for charging phones or tablets. This layout covers most laptop peripherals without forcing you to swap cables constantly.  
2. **Data speed** – Look for a hub that can handle up to 10Gbps. That speed is fast enough for external SSDs, high‑resolution webcams, and other bandwidth‑hungry devices.  
3. **Power delivery** – A built‑in 65W power adapter (20V/3.25A) ensures the hub stays powered even when multiple devices are attached. Remember, the two PD ports are intended for phones or tablets, not for powering a laptop.  
4. **Cable length** – A 3.3‑foot cable gives you enough reach to place the hub wherever it’s most convenient on your desk without creating a tangled mess.  
5. **Ergonomics** – An angled, 32‑degree design lets you plug in cables without them bending sharply, which prolongs connector life on both hub and laptop.
