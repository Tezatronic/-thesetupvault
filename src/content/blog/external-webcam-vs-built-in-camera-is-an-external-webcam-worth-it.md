### Why Built‑In Cameras Often Miss the Mark

Laptop manufacturers have gotten better at squeezing a camera into thinner chassis, but the trade‑offs are still noticeable:

- **Resolution limits** – Many integrated cameras top out at 720p, while a modest external model can deliver true 1080p at 30 fps, giving you sharper facial detail.
- **Narrow field of view** – Built‑ins usually capture a tight 60‑degree slice, which forces you to sit directly in front of the screen. A wider angle lets you frame yourself comfortably without constantly adjusting.
- **Microphone quality** – The tiny mics inside a laptop pick up a lot of desk noise and often struggle beyond a meter. External webcams typically bundle dedicated microphones that can hear you from a couple of meters away.
- **Low‑light performance** – Thin lenses and tiny sensors mean grainy footage in dim rooms. An external unit often has a larger sensor and better optics, producing a cleaner picture when the lights are low.

If you’re using video calls for casual chats, the built‑in camera may be “good enough.” But for professional meetings, teaching, streaming, or recording content, the shortcomings become frustrating fast.
