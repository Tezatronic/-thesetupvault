## Why a Dedicated Stand Matters for MacBooks

MacBooks are sleek, but that thin profile often forces you into a forward‑head posture when the screen sits low on the desk. A proper stand raises the display to eye level, reduces neck strain, and improves airflow under the chassis—something especially helpful for the higher‑performance M‑series chips that can heat up during long editing sessions.

Beyond ergonomics, a stand creates a clean work surface. With the laptop lifted, you can place an external keyboard and mouse at the perfect height, turning a single‑device setup into a true desktop experience without sacrificing the MacBook’s portability.

## Key Factors to Consider When Picking a Stand for Your MacBook

| Factor | What to Look For |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Material** | Aluminum alloy gives a premium feel, dissipates heat better than plastic, and matches the MacBook’s own chassis. |
| **Weight Capacity** | Make sure the stand can comfortably support the weight of your MacBook model—up to 11 lb covers the entire 10‑to‑17.3 in range. |
| **Height Adjustability** | A range from about 5.7 in to 8.1 in lets you line the screen up with your eye level whether you’re seated or standing at a sit‑stand desk. |
| **Angle & Rotation** | Adjustable tilt from 45° (for typing) to 180° (flat) plus 360° base rotation gives flexibility for presentations, drawing, or sharing the screen. |
| **Portability** | A stand that weighs around 2.3 lb is easy to lift when you need to move it to a coffee shop or a different room. |
| **Laptop Size Compatibility** | Ensure it fits the 10‑to‑17.3 in footprint of your MacBook, including the larger 16‑inch models. |
| **Stability** | A wide base and non‑slip pads keep the stand steady even when you type vigorously. |
| **Cable Management** | Look for a design that leaves space for the USB‑C power cable or external monitors without crowding the desk. |

## Setting Up for Optimal Ergonomics

1. **Raise the screen to eye level.** Adjust the height until the top of the MacBook display is at or slightly below your eye line when you sit upright.
2. **Tilt for typing.** Set the angle around 70°–80° for comfortable key presses; switch to a flatter angle when you’re watching videos or presenting.
3. **Align the keyboard.** Keep the built‑in keyboard centered under the screen, then place an external keyboard at the same height as your elbows to maintain a neutral wrist posture.
4. **Mind the cables.** Route the power cable behind the stand’s open frame and use a short Velcro tie or a reusable cable tie (see our guide on [The Best Reusable Cable Ties for Desk Cable Management](/blog/the-best-reusable-cable-ties-for-desk-cable-management-a-practical-buying-guide/)) to keep it out of the way.
