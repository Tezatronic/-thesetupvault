### Why a USB‑C Hub with PD Matters

Modern laptops and tablets rely on USB‑C for everything—from video output to fast storage. A hub that simply expands ports can quickly become a bottleneck if it can’t keep up with the data rates you expect. Adding Power Delivery (PD) changes the game: you can keep a phone or tablet topped up without hunting for a wall charger, and you still have high‑speed lanes for external SSDs, webcams, or a mouse.

In a typical home‑office layout, the hub sits between your laptop and the desk peripherals. The convenience of a single cable that both powers a device and handles up to **10 Gbps** data transfer means fewer cords, a cleaner look, and less chance of accidentally unplugging the wrong thing during a video call.

### Setting Up the Hub in a Home‑Office Desk

1. **Position the hub near the back edge of your desk** so the angled connector is easy to reach but doesn’t block other ports on your laptop.  
2. **Route the 3.3‑foot cable through a cable‑management sleeve** or a desk grommet. This keeps the cord out of sight and reduces the chance of snagging.  
3. **Plug the 65 W adapter into a wall outlet** that’s close enough to avoid a long power cord stretch. The adapter’s size is modest, so it can sit on a power strip without crowding other plugs.  
4. **Connect your high‑speed devices first** (external SSD, webcam) to the USB‑C 3.2 or USB‑A 3.2 ports. Then attach lower‑speed peripherals (mouse, keyboard) to the USB‑A 3.0 ports.  
5. **Reserve the two USB‑C PD ports for mobile devices**. Place your phone on a stand or a charging dock nearby, and let the hub handle the charge while you work.

- **Only two high‑speed USB‑C/USB‑A 3.2 ports** – While the hub offers nine ports total, only three of them (the single USB‑C 3.2 and the two USB‑A 3.2) support the full 10 Gbps speed.
