## Key Considerations When Buying a Computer Monitor Stand

Choosing an adjustable monitor riser requires balancing physical ergonomics with practical desk management. Before buying, evaluate these four main factors:

### 1. Height Adjustability and Ergonomic Alignment
Ergonomic guidelines state that your neck should remain neutral while looking at your screen. If your monitor lacks a height-adjustable stock stand, placing it flat on your desk forces you to tilt your head downward. A good computer monitor stand allows you to modify its leg height so you can hit your exact eye level. Look for leg systems with secure locking mechanisms—such as pin-lock or screw-in extension legs—that won't wobble or collapse under daily pressure.

### 2. Weight Capacity and Structural Integrity
Monitors, especially heavy ultrawides or all-in-one desktop systems, create concentrated downward pressure. Cheap plastic risers tend to bow in the middle over time, creating an unstable surface and risking damage to your equipment. Solid metal, particularly steel or heavy-gauge aluminum, offers the best long-term stability. Ensure the load limit comfortably exceeds the combined weight of your display and any secondary gear placed on top.

### 3. Airflow and Surface Ventilation
If you plan to store a closed laptop, dock, or external hard drive underneath your riser, thermal management becomes a real priority. Solid wood or solid acrylic platforms trap hot exhaust air, causing electronics stored beneath them to run hot. A perforated or mesh metal platform encourages natural convection, drawing cool air up and keeping your primary work tools cool.

### 4. Footprint vs. Usable Under-Desk Space
A riser should save desk space, not consume it. Look for designs with streamlined legs that maximize the open clearance beneath the shelf. This allows you to tuck away a compact keyboard, a stream deck, or a notebook when you need clear space to write. ## How to Ergonomically Position Your Monitor

Adding an adjustable monitor riser is only the first step; placing it correctly ensures you get the full health benefits of an upgraded workstation.

1. **Center the Screen:** Place the riser directly in front of your primary seated position. Looking off to the side for hours creates asymmetric muscle tension in your neck and upper back.
2. **Set the Eyeline:** Adjust your chair so your feet are flat on the floor and your knees are bent at a 90-degree angle. Look straight ahead. Your eyes should align naturally with the top top 2 to 3 inches of the screen area.
3. **Maintain Arm’s-Length Distance:** Sit back in your normal working posture and extend your arm straight out. Your fingertips should just touch the glass of your monitor. If it is closer, you risk eye strain; if it is farther, you may find yourself leaning forward and hunching your shoulders.
4. **Manage Rear Cables:** A open riser leaves rear ports exposed. Run power and display cables directly down the back of the riser legs and bundle them using adjustable cord straps to keep your workspace visual clutter-free. Using dedicated [adjustable cord straps](/blog/how-to-organize-desk-cables-with-adjustable-cord-straps/) prevents cables from sagging down into your under-stand storage space.
