## Why Material and Build Quality Matter for MacBooks

Apple hardware features a precision-machined aluminum chassis. Placing a MacBook on a cheap plastic stand often creates an unstable surface, and the aesthetic contrast stands out on a clean desk. 

An **aluminum laptop stand** offers two distinct functional advantages over plastic alternatives:

1. **Passive Heat Dissipation:** Aluminum acts as a natural heat sink. While modern Apple silicon runs cooler than older Intel models, heavy export workflows, rendering, or extended external display usage still generate heat through the bottom casing. Aluminum alloy surfaces pull warmth away from the underside of the laptop.
2. **Structural Rigidity:** MacBooks have dense unibody cases with a center of gravity that shifts when the lid is pushed back past 100 degrees. Flimsier stands flex or tip under that leverage. A solid metal stand keeps the platform anchored.

When evaluating materials, make sure the stand features non-slip silicone pads anywhere metal meets metal. You want rubberized grips along the resting ledge and base plate to protect your MacBook's anodized finish from scuffs.

## Key Adjustability Features to Look For

Fixed-height risers work well if your desk height and torso length happen to align with the factory measurement, but adjustable stands provide far more versatility. 

### Height Range
A proper ergonomic setup positions the top third of your MacBook display at or slightly below eye level when you sit upright. Look for a mechanism that offers a range between roughly 5.7 inches and 8.1 inches of vertical adjustment. This range allows you to match the laptop screen height to a secondary external monitor for seamless side-by-side cursor tracking.

### Tilt and Angle Flexibility
Adjusting the tilt angle between 45 to 180 degrees lets you angle the keyboard deck back and adjust the webcam angle. Angling the laptop camera slightly down or straight-on produces a much better view on video meetings than shooting upward from desk level.

### Base Swivel
A 360-degree rotating base is one of the most practical additions to modern **office desk accessories**. Swivel functionality lets you pivot the screen to share a presentation with a coworker or adjust for midday window glare without dragging the base across your desk surface.

## Ergonomics and the Peripherals You Need

Using a **laptop stand for MacBook** requires a shift in how you interact with your machine. Elevating the laptop puts the built-in keyboard and trackpad at an awkward angle for direct typing, which bends your wrists upward and causes fatigue.

To build an ergonomic station around your stand:
- **Add an external input setup:** Pair your elevated MacBook with an external mouse and a [silent wireless keyboard](/blog/why-a-silent-wireless-keyboard-belongs-in-your-shared-workspace/) so your hands rest at a comfortable 90-degree elbow angle on the desk.
- **Surface protection:** Placing your stand and input devices over a [PU leather desk pad](/blog/pu-leather-desk-pad-vs-plain-desk-mat-which-surface-wins-for-your-home-office/) protects your desk surface from heavy hardware bases and gives your mouse a uniform tracking area.
- **Port management:** Elevating the MacBook raises its side ports off the desk. Using a dedicated [USB-C multiport hub](/blog/what-to-know-about-usb-c-multiport-hubs-with-pd-charging/) keeps cables organized below desk level rather than hanging down from the stand.
