### Why the material matters

When you’re building a desk that will see daily plugging and unplugging, the hub’s chassis is more than a cosmetic choice. Plastic housings can warp over time, especially when the hub is left on for hours and the internal components heat up. Aluminum, on the other hand, acts like a tiny heat sink. It spreads warmth across a larger surface, keeping the silicon inside cooler and reducing the chance of throttling during long file transfers.

Beyond heat, aluminum feels solid in the hand. The weight gives it a sense of permanence that discourages accidental knocks. A plastic hub may feel light enough to slide off the edge of a narrow desk, while an aluminum unit stays put, especially when paired with a 32‑degree angled ergonomic design that tucks the ports into a comfortable position.

Aesthetic-wise, many modern desk setups favor a metal look that matches laptop bodies, monitor stands, and other accessories. A sleek aluminum hub can blend in without looking like a cheap add‑on.

### Key factors to compare when choosing a hub for your desk

| Factor | What to look for in aluminum | What to watch for in plastic |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Build quality** | One‑piece unibody, rigid, resistant to flex | May have seams or snap‑fit joints that loosen |
| **Heat management** | Metal body dissipates heat, keeps data rates stable | Can become warm, potentially throttling USB‑3.2 speeds |
| **Port variety** | Often includes a mix of USB‑C and USB‑A, sometimes PD ports | May limit you to fewer or older‑generation ports |
| **Data speed** | Supports up to 10 Gbps on USB‑3.2 ports | May claim the same speed but struggle under load |
| **Power delivery** | Integrated power adapter can supply consistent wattage | Relies on the host’s power, which can be insufficient |
| **Cable length** | Longer cables (e.g., 3.3 feet) give flexibility on larger desks | Shorter cables can be restrictive |
| **Ergonomics** | Angled designs keep plugs accessible without bending | Flat designs may force you to reach over the hub |
| **Price** | Typically higher due to material and engineering | Usually cheaper, but you may pay later in replacements |
