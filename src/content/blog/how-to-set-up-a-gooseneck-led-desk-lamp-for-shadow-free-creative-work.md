When you need a **desk lamp for drawing**, the most reliable way to avoid shadows is to mount a gooseneck LED lamp so the light source sits directly above your work surface and points straight down at a slight angle.

Below is a practical, step‑by‑step guide that shows you how to turn the **Airlonv LED Desk Lamp with Gooseneck Clamp** into a reliable source of **task lighting for crafts**, **LED lamp for artists**, and **creative workspace lighting**. The instructions assume you already have the lamp on hand and a typical home‑office desk.

---

## 1. Pick the Right Clamp Location

1. **Identify the thickest part of your desk** – the clamp on the Airlonv fits desktops up to 2.36 inches thick, so any standard desk will work.  
2. **Place the clamp on the side opposite your dominant hand**. This keeps the arm out of the way while you sketch or cut.  
3. **Tighten the metal clamp securely**. A firm grip prevents the lamp from slipping when you adjust the neck.

*Why it matters*: A stable clamp means you can move the gooseneck without worrying about wobble, which is essential when you’re working on fine details.

---

## 2. Set the Color Temperature for Your Medium

The lamp offers a **color temperature range from 2700 K (warm) to 6500 K (daylight)**, and you can adjust it continuously.

| Medium | Recommended Temp |
|--------|------------------|
| Pencil/ink drawing | 3500 K – 4000 K (neutral) |
| Digital illustration (color work) | 5000 K – 5500 K (daylight) |
| Crafting with fabric or yarn | 3000 K – 3500 K (warm, reduces strain) |

Turn the temperature dial until the light feels natural for the colors you’re using. A neutral tone helps you see true hues, while a warmer tone is easier on the eyes for long sessions.

---

## 3. Adjust Brightness Before You Start

The Airlonv lamp provides **stepless dimming from 1 % to 100 %**. For most creative tasks, aim for a brightness that fills the entire work area without causing glare on glossy paper.

*Tip*: Start at about 70 % and lower it if you notice reflections. Because the lamp is **USB powered (5 V/3 A adapter included)**, you can plug it into a hub or directly into a wall outlet without adding extra load to your desk.

---

## 4. Position the 17‑Inch Light Bar for Even Coverage

The lamp’s **17‑inch wide light bar** spreads light across a large surface. Follow these steps:

1. **Pull the gooseneck upward** until the bar hovers a few inches above the desk.  
2. **Center the bar over the area you’ll be working on** – for a sketchbook, align the bar with the middle of the page.  
3. **Tilt the bar forward just enough** so the light hits the surface at a 45‑degree angle. This angle minimizes shadows while keeping the light from reflecting off the page.

If you switch tasks (e.g., from drawing to cutting paper), simply slide the bar left or right and re‑tilt as needed. The flexible neck makes micro‑adjustments painless.

---

## 5. Fine‑Tune the Neck Angle for Specific Tasks

Different creative activities benefit from slightly different angles:

| Task | Ideal Neck Angle |
|------|-----------------|
| Detailed line work | Slightly steeper (closer to vertical) |
| Large‑format painting | Flatter (more horizontal) |
| Crafting with scissors or knives | Slightly angled away from the cutting edge to avoid glare |

Experiment by moving the neck in small increments. The **100 LED beads** have a **60,000‑hour lifespan**, so you won’t need to worry about premature dimming while you’re perfecting the angle.

---

## 6. Keep Cables Tidy

Since the lamp runs on USB, you’ll have a short power cord. Use a **cable clip or a Velcro tie** to route the cable behind the desk leg or along the edge of the clamp. This prevents accidental tugging and keeps your creative workspace uncluttered.

If you already use a USB hub for peripherals, you can plug the lamp into the hub’s power‑delivery port. For more on managing USB power on a desk, see our guide on [USB‑C hubs with PD charging](/blog/what-to-know-about-usb-c-hubs-with-pd-charging-for-desk-setups/).

---

## 7. Test for Shadows

Before you settle in for a session:

1. **Place a piece of white paper under the lamp**.  
2. **Hold a pen at the edge of the paper** and move it around.  
3. **Observe any dark patches**. If you see shadows, adjust the tilt or raise the bar a few millimeters.

A quick test saves you from discovering a shadow mid‑project.

---

## 8. Integrate Lighting Into Your Overall Desk Layout

Creative work rarely happens in isolation. Think about where your monitor, keyboard, and storage sit relative to the lamp. Position the lamp so it doesn’t cast light on your screen (which can cause glare) and leaves enough room for your hand movements.
