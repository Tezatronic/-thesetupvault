## Key Factors When Choosing an Adjustable Monitor Riser

A dedicated computer monitor stand does more than just prop up your screen. It dictates your sitting posture, clears desk clutter, and ensures your screen remains steady while typing. When shopping for an adjustable monitor riser, focus on these core criteria:

### 1. Weight Capacity and Structural Rigidity
Most modern 24-inch to 32-inch monitors weigh between 8 and 25 pounds, but when you add a heavy factory stand base or a secondary hub, weight adds up quickly. Cheap plastic risers tend to bow in the center over time, leading to an unstable screen. 

Look for heavy-duty metal construction that can comfortably handle the weight of larger displays without sagging. A stand that supports substantial weight gives you peace of mind if you later upgrade from an office monitor to a heavier ultrawide or an all-in-one desktop.

### 2. Platform Dimensions vs. Desk Depth
Desk depth varies widely in home offices. If you work on a shallow 24-inch deep desk, a bulky wooden riser will consume too much room, pushing your keyboard to the edge. 

A platform length around 14 to 15 inches and a depth between 9 and 10 inches fits virtually all standard desktop monitor bases while leaving plenty of room in front for a keyboard and a desk mat. If you use a dual-screen setup with a laptop, understanding the difference between a [laptop stand vs riser](/blog/laptop-stand-vs-riser-which-one-do-you-actually-need/) will help you determine whether you need a dedicated screen shelf or an angled tray.

### 3. Surface Stability and Scratch Protection
A stable base is essential. Typing vigorously shouldn't cause your screen to rattle. Look for a stand built with anti-skid rubber pads on the feet. These pads serve two purposes: they prevent the stand from sliding when you adjust cables, and they protect wood or laminate desk finishes from scratches.

### 4. Under-Stand Clearance
Reclaiming desk real estate is one of the biggest reasons to get an adjustable monitor riser. The open space beneath the platform should be wide and tall enough to slide a compact mechanical keyboard, a notebook, an audio interface, or a docking station out of the way when the workday ends.

## How to Set Up Your Riser for Proper Ergonomics

Getting a stand is only step one; setting it up properly makes the real difference:

1. **Align Screen Height:** Place your monitor on the stand so that the top third of the screen is level with your horizontal eye line. When looking straight ahead, your neck should remain relaxed in a neutral posture.
2. **Set Viewing Distance:** The screen should sit approximately an arm's length away from your seating position. If the stand is 9.3 inches deep, position it toward the rear edge of your desk to maintain sufficient distance.
3. **Manage Desk Surfaces:** Place the anti-skid rubber pads firmly against the desktop surface. If you use a large desk pad, make sure all four feet rest evenly on or off the mat surface to prevent any micro-wobble while typing.
