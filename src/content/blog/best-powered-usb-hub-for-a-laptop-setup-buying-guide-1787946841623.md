## What Makes a Powered USB Hub Worth Buying for a Laptop?

### 1. Port mix that matches a laptop’s needs  
Most modern laptops ship with only a couple of USB‑C ports and maybe one USB‑A. A hub that offers a blend of USB‑C and USB‑A lets you connect external monitors, SSDs, keyboards, mice, and legacy devices without constantly swapping cables.

### 2. Real power delivery, not just a data pass‑through  
A powered hub includes its own adapter, so the hub can supply enough juice to keep high‑draw peripherals (like external hard drives) alive while the laptop stays focused on processing. Look for a hub that comes with a robust power brick; the intpw includes a 65 W (20 V/3.25 A) adapter, which is more than enough for most desk‑side gear.

### 3. Speed that keeps up with fast storage  
If you regularly move large files or run apps from an external SSD, you’ll notice the difference between USB 2.0 and USB 3.2. A hub that can push up to 10 Gbps on its USB‑C and USB‑A 3.2 ports ensures you won’t be bottlenecked by the hub itself.

### 4. Build quality that survives daily use  
A solid aluminum chassis feels premium, dissipates heat better than plastic, and resists the occasional bump. An ergonomic angle (the intpw’s 32‑degree tilt) also helps keep cables from sagging and makes plugging in devices a smoother motion.

### 5. Cable length that fits your desk layout  
A 3.3‑foot cable gives you flexibility to place the hub wherever it’s most convenient—right next to the laptop, or tucked behind a monitor arm.

### 6. Clear limits on power‑delivery ports  
Many hubs advertise “PD” ports that can charge laptops, but not all can handle the wattage a laptop needs. Knowing the hub’s PD ports are intended for phones and tablets avoids a nasty surprise when you try to power a heavy‑duty laptop.

These criteria form the backbone of any sensible purchase decision. If a hub meets most of them, you’re likely to enjoy a smoother, more reliable workstation.
