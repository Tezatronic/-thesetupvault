### Why Port Count Matters

When you start adding a second monitor, a webcam, a keyboard, a mouse, a few USB‑C dongles, and perhaps a couple of external SSDs, the built‑in ports on a laptop or desktop disappear fast. Running out of ports forces you to constantly unplug and replug devices, which interrupts workflow and can even lead to data corruption if a drive is removed mid‑transfer.

A hub with too few ports becomes a bottleneck, while a hub with far more ports than you’ll ever use adds unnecessary cost and desk clutter. The sweet spot depends on three practical questions:

1. **What devices do you connect daily?**  
   - Monitors (via USB‑C or DisplayLink adapters)  
   - External storage (SSD/HDD)  
   - Input devices (keyboard, mouse, drawing tablet)  
   - Charging needs (phone, tablet)  

2. **Do you need high‑speed data for any of those devices?**  
   - Video‑capture cards, fast SSDs, and docking stations benefit from USB 3.2’s up to 10 Gbps bandwidth.  

3. **Will you ever charge a laptop from the hub?**  
   - Most hubs, including the intpw model, provide PD ports that are sized for phones and tablets, not for laptop charging.

Answering these questions narrows the ideal port count to a range that matches your workflow without overpaying.

| **Device Mix** | Count how many USB‑A, USB‑C, and charging ports you need. | A hub that mixes both types reduces the need for separate adapters. |

| **Power Delivery (PD)** | PD ports for phones/tablets, not laptops. | Knowing the PD limitation avoids the mistake of trying to power a laptop from a hub that can’t supply enough wattage. |

| **Build Quality** | Aluminum unibody with a 32‑degree angled design. | A sturdy chassis resists wobble and the angle keeps the hub from blocking adjacent ports. |
| **Future‑Proofing** | Extra ports for upcoming peripherals. | Buying a hub with a couple of spare ports can save you a future upgrade. |

### How to Match Port Count to Your Setup

1. **Map your current devices.** Write down each peripheral and its connector type.  
2. **Identify high‑speed needs.** If you have an external SSD or a video capture device, earmark a USB‑C 3.2 or USB‑A 3.2 port for it.  
3. **Add a buffer.** Add one or two extra ports to your tally for future gear.
