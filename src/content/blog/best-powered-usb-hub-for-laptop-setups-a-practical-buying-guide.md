## Why a Powered Hub Matters for a Laptop Desk

A laptop’s built‑in ports are often a compromise: a few USB‑C connectors, maybe a single USB‑A, and limited power delivery. When you add a monitor, external SSD, mouse, keyboard, and a phone charger, you quickly run out of space and bandwidth. A powered hub solves two problems at once:

1. **Extra ports without sacrificing speed** – the hub’s USB‑C 3.2 and USB‑A 3.2 ports keep data transfers at up to 10 Gbps, which is essential for large files or 4K video streams.
2. **Stable power for bus‑powered devices** – the included 65 W adapter guarantees that even power‑hungry peripherals stay responsive, preventing the dreaded “insufficient power” warnings.

## Key Factors to Consider When Choosing a Powered USB Hub

### 1. Power rating and adapter quality  
A hub that draws power from the laptop can quickly overload its internal supply. Look for a model that ships with its own high‑wattage adapter. The intpw hub includes a 65 W (20 V/3.25 A) power brick, which is more than enough for most laptop‑side peripherals.

### 2. Port mix and future‑proofing  
Your workflow may involve a mix of USB‑C and USB‑A devices. A good hub offers both, ideally with the latest standards. The intpw hub provides:

- 1 USB‑C 3.2 data port  
- 2 USB‑A 3.2 data ports  
- 4 USB‑A 3.0 ports for legacy gear  
- 2 USB‑C PD ports (45 W each) for charging phones or tablets  

That spread means you can keep a mouse, keyboard, external drive, and a phone charger all plugged in simultaneously.

### 3. Data throughput  
If you regularly move large media files, you’ll notice the difference between USB‑2.0 and USB‑3.2. A hub that caps out at 5 Gbps can become a bottleneck. The intpw hub’s 10 Gbps capability keeps transfers swift.

### 4. Build quality and ergonomics  
A metal unibody feels sturdier than plastic and resists wobble on a busy desk. The 32‑degree angled design of the intpw hub lets cables sit naturally without bending sharply, reducing strain on connectors.

### 5. Cable length and placement flexibility  
A 3.3‑foot cable gives you enough reach to place the hub behind a monitor or on a side table while keeping the power brick out of the way.

### 6. Compatibility with your laptop’s charging needs  
Most laptops draw more than 45 W, so a hub’s PD ports are usually meant for phones or tablets, not for powering the host laptop itself. Keep that in mind when planning your power budget.
