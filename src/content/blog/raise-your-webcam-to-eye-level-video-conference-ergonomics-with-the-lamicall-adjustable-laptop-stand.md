## Why Webcam Height Matters

During a Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet session, the angle of your camera has a huge impact on how you’re perceived. A low‑angle shot can make you look cramped, force you to crane your neck, and cast unflattering shadows. Raising the camera to eye level:

* Keeps your neck in a neutral position, reducing strain during long meetings.  
* Shows your face straight on, which reads as more confident and engaged.  
* Allows natural light from a window or desk lamp to illuminate your face evenly.

Most built‑in laptop webcams sit at the bottom edge of the screen, so without a lift, you’re stuck with a sub‑optimal angle.

## Setting Up the Stand for a Professional Look

### 1. Position the Stand

Place the stand in the center of your desk, directly in front of your primary light source (a window or a desk lamp). Because the base rotates 360°, you can fine‑tune the orientation without moving the whole desk.

### 2. Adjust Height and Tilt

* Raise the platform to the top of the height range.  
* Tilt the screen back about 10‑15°; this pushes the webcam forward and aligns it with your eyes.  
* If you use an external microphone, keep it just below the laptop to avoid picking up desk noise.

### 3. Optimize Lighting

With the laptop higher, you have more room to place a light source above or to the side. A soft‑box or an LED desk lamp placed at a 45° angle will illuminate your face evenly and reduce harsh shadows. Because the stand’s angle can be set as low as 45°, you can also angle the screen to reflect light away from the webcam, preventing glare.

### 4. Manage Cables

A tidy cable run keeps the background clean on video. Pair the stand with a powered USB‑C hub for your webcam, headphones, and any external peripherals. Our guide on [how to set up a powered USB hub for video‑conferencing gear](/blog/how-to-set-up-a-powered-usb-hub-for-video-conferencing-gear/) walks you through the best layout.

### 5. Test Before the Call

Open your video‑call app, enable the preview, and make sure the webcam shows you from the shoulders up with a neutral background. Adjust the tilt or rotation if the top of your head is cut off or if the screen reflects too much light.
