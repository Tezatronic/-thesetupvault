When you work at a desk for hours, the simplest way to protect your neck and back is to **place the light source at eye level, slightly above and in front of your monitor, with the beam angled down toward the work surface**. This positioning eliminates glare, keeps your head upright, and lets you maintain a neutral spine while you type, write, or read.

Below is a practical guide for positioning the **Airlonv LED Desk Lamp with Gooseneck Clamp** so it becomes an ergonomic ally rather than a decorative afterthought.

## Why Lamp Placement Matters for Ergonomics

- **Reduces forward head tilt.** When the light is too low, you instinctively crane your neck forward to see details, increasing strain on cervical vertebrae.
- **Minimizes glare on screens.** A well‑placed lamp shines on the keyboard and documents without reflecting off the monitor, letting you keep the screen at a comfortable brightness.
- **Supports a neutral wrist posture.** Proper illumination lets you keep your forearms close to the desk, avoiding the “reach‑over‑the‑light” posture that stresses shoulders.

## Step‑by‑Step Positioning Guide

### 1. Attach the Lamp Securely
Clamp the base to the edge of your desk where the lamp can swing freely. The metal clamp’s capacity for desks up to 2.36 inches thick means it works on most standard workstations and even on thicker tabletop surfaces.

### 2. Set the Height
Raise the lamp so the center of the 17‑inch light bar sits **just above eye level** when you’re seated upright. For most people, this means the light bar will be roughly 2–3 inches higher than the top of the monitor.

### 3. Angle the Beam
Bend the gooseneck so the light falls **45 degrees down** onto the keyboard and any paperwork. The goal is a soft wash of light that covers the work area without spilling directly onto the screen.

### 4. Adjust Color Temperature
- **Warm (2700 K–3500 K):** Use for evening work or when you want a relaxed atmosphere.  
- **Neutral (4000 K–5000 K):** Ideal for daytime tasks that require color accuracy, such as graphic design.  
- **Cool (5500 K–6500 K):** Best for early‑morning focus or reading fine print.

Start at a mid‑range (around 4000 K) and tweak based on comfort. The stepless dimming lets you fine‑tune brightness without flicker, which is crucial for preventing eye fatigue that can translate into neck strain.

### 5. Test for Glare
Sit at your normal working distance and glance at the monitor. If you see reflections, slightly shift the lamp outward or lower the angle a few degrees. Small adjustments can make a big difference.

### 6. Secure the Gooseneck
Once you’ve found the sweet spot, gently tighten the gooseneck joint. The lamp’s design holds its shape well, but a loose joint will drift over time, forcing you to readjust and potentially disrupting your posture.

## Integrating the Lamp with Your Desk Layout

- **Monitor height:** Top of the screen should be at or just below eye level.  
- **Keyboard and mouse:** Keep them close enough that elbows stay at a 90‑degree angle.  
- **Chair:** Adjust seat height so feet rest flat on the floor.

## Fine‑Tuning Brightness for Long Sessions

Long hours of screen work can cause subtle eye strain that manifests as neck tension. Here’s a quick method:

1. **Start at 30 % brightness** on the lamp while your monitor is at a comfortable level.  
2. **Every 30 minutes, reduce the lamp by 5 %** if you’re not reading fine details.  
3. **Increase to 70 %** only when you need to see small fonts or intricate graphics.
