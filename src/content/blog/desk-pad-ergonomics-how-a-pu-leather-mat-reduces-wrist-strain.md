## Contact Stress: The Overlooked Cause of Wrist Fatigue

Contact stress occurs whenever your body presses against a hard, unyielding edge or flat plane. In a home office, the most common point of friction is the edge of your desk, followed closely by the unpadded desktop directly beneath your keyboard and mouse. 

When you rest your forearms or the base of your palms on a bare surface, your skeleton presses outward through thin muscle and tendon sheaths against an unyielding object. Over hours of typing, this constant compression leads to that familiar dull ache in the forearms and tingling in the outer fingers.

A PU leather surface introduces just enough compliance to distribute that downward force. While not squishy like foam, high-density PU leather yields slightly under direct pressure, spreading the load across a broader surface area of your skin. 

More importantly, pulling the pad slightly forward so it sits flush with or overhangs the front desk edge by a millimeter rounds off the sharp 90-degree corner that typically bites into your forearms during keyboard work. If you pair this with proper switch selection—such as comparing [mechanical vs membrane wireless keyboards for office use](/blog/mechanical-vs-membrane-wireless-keyboards-for-office-use-which-is-right-for-you/) to minimize key-bottoming shock—the reduction in physical fatigue at the end of the day is immediate.

## Mouse Glide Surface and Arm-Driven Tracking

Ergonomic mouse control is not just about the mouse itself; it is determined by the friction of the surface beneath it. On bare desks, natural oils from your skin and microscopic imperfections in the wood or laminate create inconsistent dynamic friction. Some spots feel slick, while others feel tacky. This inconsistency forces the tiny stabilizing muscles in your wrist and fingers to constantly micro-adjust, leading to tension headaches and forearm cramping.

A uniform PU leather top creates a stable mouse glide surface that balances static friction (the force needed to start moving) and dynamic friction (the force required to keep moving). 

1. **Predictable resistance:** The low, uniform texture lets optical sensors track smoothly without skipping, while providing sufficient tactile feedback to stop the cursor precisely where you want it.
2. **Encouraging whole-arm movement:** Because the pad offers a consistent, wide area, it encourages you to pivot from your elbow and shoulder rather than flicking your wrist back and forth. Pivoting at the elbow keeps the wrist joint neutral, transferring mechanical work to larger muscle groups that are built to handle prolonged activity.
3. **No catching on seams:** Traditional small mouse pads create an artificial border. When your mouse or palm slides off the edge of a tiny pad onto the desk, your wrist hitches. An extended mat provides an unbroken field across your main work area.

For those running a [silent wireless keyboard and adjustable DPI mouse](/blog/silent-wireless-keyboard-and-adjustable-dpi-mouse-for-shared-workspaces-1788814273162/), setting your mouse to a moderate DPI and gliding across a smooth surface lets you execute broad screen sweeps without lifting or twisting the joint.

## Potential Drawbacks

While an extended PU leather pad solves everyday contact stress and tracking inconsistency, it is not a universal solution for every ergonomic issue.

### Not a Replacement for a Dedicated High-Rise Wrist Rest
If you type on a high-profile mechanical keyboard with tall keycaps, a flat desk pad will not bridge the vertical gap between the desk and the home row. A desk pad softens the desk surface, but it is thin and flat. Workers dealing with severe wrist drop or active tendon inflammation will still require a dedicated wedge rest to eliminate upward bending (dorsiflexion).

### Firmness Over Cushioning
PU leather is resilient, easy to wipe down, and waterproof, but it is not memory foam. If your primary goal is squishy, cloud-like padding beneath your elbows, a thick wool felt mat or a dedicated gel rest will provide more vertical give. The Aothia pad is engineered for posture stabilization and smooth tracking, offering a firm dampening effect rather than plush cushioning.
