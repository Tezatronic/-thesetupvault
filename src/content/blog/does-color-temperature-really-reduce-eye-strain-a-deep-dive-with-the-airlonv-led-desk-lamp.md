### Why Color Temperature Matters

When you stare at a screen for hours, the light that surrounds you either adds to the fatigue or helps your eyes relax. Here’s how:

| Factor | How it impacts eye strain |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Warm light (≈2700 K)** | Mimics sunrise/sunset, reduces glare, and is easier on the eyes in low‑light environments. |
| **Cool light (≈6500 K)** | Approximates daylight, improves contrast for detailed tasks but can feel harsher if over‑bright. |
| **Blue‑rich light** | Triggers the brain’s alertness response; excessive exposure late in the day can disrupt circadian rhythm and increase perceived strain. |
| **Dimming range** | Allows you to match illumination to the task and ambient light, preventing the eyes from constantly adjusting. |

Most modern LED desk lamps, including the Airlonv model, let you slide smoothly between 2700 K and 6500 K with stepless dimming from 1 % to 100 %. This flexibility means you can start the day with a cooler, brighter setting for focused work, then shift to a warmer, softer glow for reading or winding down.

### Key Factors to Consider Beyond Temperature

1. **Brightness vs. Temperature**  
   A lamp set to a “cool” temperature but dimmed too low can force your eyes to strain to see details. Conversely, a warm lamp that’s too bright can cause glare. Aim for a balance: keep the light level just enough to illuminate your work surface without washing out the screen.

2. **Positioning of the Light Source**  
   The gooseneck design lets you angle the light precisely. Position the lamp so the beam falls on your keyboard and documents, not directly into your eyes or onto the monitor. A 17‑inch wide light bar spreads illumination evenly across a typical desk.

3. **Ambient Lighting**  
   Even with a perfect desk lamp, a completely dark room will make the screen’s contrast feel harsher. Adding a low‑intensity background light (e.g., a floor lamp or soft wall wash) can reduce the overall visual jump between screen and surroundings.

4. **Personal Sensitivity**  
   Some people are more sensitive to blue light than others. If you notice headaches after late‑night sessions, experiment with warmer settings after 6 p.m. and keep the lamp’s brightness low.

5. **Power Source and Consistency**  
   The Airlonv lamp runs on a 10 W USB adapter (5 V/3 A) that’s included, delivering stable output without flicker—a common cause of subtle eye fatigue.

By weighing these factors together, you’ll get a clearer picture of how much the lamp’s color temperature actually contributes to comfort.

### How to Use the Lamp for Minimum Eye Strain

1. **Start the day cool and bright** – Set the temperature around 5000 K–6000 K and dim to about 70 % of maximum. This mimics natural daylight and helps you stay alert.  
2. **Mid‑day adjustment** – As ambient light changes, lower the brightness a bit and move toward a neutral 4000 K setting.  
3. **Evening wind‑down** – Switch to 2700 K–3000 K and dim down to 30 % or less. This reduces blue light exposure and signals your brain that it’s time to relax.  
4. **Position the gooseneck** – Aim the light so it illuminates your keyboard and documents without shining directly into your eyes. A slight angle that casts a soft pool of light on the work surface works best.  
5. **Pair with ambient lighting** – Add a low‑intensity floor lamp or a smart bulb set to a warm hue to eliminate stark contrast between the screen and the surrounding darkness.

Following this routine, you’ll notice fewer headaches, less dryness, and a smoother transition between tasks.
