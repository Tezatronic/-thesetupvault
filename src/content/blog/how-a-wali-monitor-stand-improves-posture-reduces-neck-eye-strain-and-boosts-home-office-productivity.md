### Why Height Matters for Ergonomics

Most people set their monitor directly on the desk, which forces the eyes to look down at a slight angle. Over the course of a workday, that downward gaze can:

* **Tighten the neck muscles** – the head tilts forward, creating a lever effect that strains the cervical spine.  
* **Increase shoulder tension** – shoulders round forward to compensate for the lowered screen.  
* **Trigger eye fatigue** – looking down reduces the effective viewing distance and forces the eyes to refocus constantly.

The ergonomic sweet spot is to have the top of the screen roughly at or just below eye level, so the neck stays in a neutral, relaxed position. Raising the monitor also lets you keep the keyboard and mouse at a comfortable height, encouraging a more natural wrist posture.

### Easing Eye Strain with Proper Height

Eye strain often stems from improper viewing distance and angle. When a monitor sits too low, you’re forced to look down, which narrows the visual field and makes the eyes work harder to maintain focus. Raising the monitor to eye level:

* **Keeps the gaze level** – the eyes stay in a natural, relaxed position.  
* **Improves distance** – you can sit a comfortable arm’s length away without craning.  
* **Reduces glare** – a higher screen is less likely to catch desk‑level lighting reflections.

### Quick Setup Checklist

1. **Clear the Desk Surface** – Remove any clutter that could interfere with the stand’s rubber pads.  
2. **Place the Stand** – Center it on the desk, ensuring the anti‑skid pads sit flat.  
3. **Position the Monitor** – Gently set the monitor on the platform; adjust the tilt if needed (most monitors have a built‑in tilt mechanism).  
4. **Check Eye Level** – Sit back in your chair, look straight ahead, and confirm the top of the screen aligns with your eyes.  
5. **Fine‑Tune Keyboard Height** – If the keyboard feels too high, consider a low‑profile keyboard tray or a separate wrist rest.
