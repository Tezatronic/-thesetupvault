### Why Field of View Matters

When you sit in front of a webcam, the camera captures everything inside its viewing angle. A narrow FOV (for example, 60°) will tightly frame your face, cutting off any background and making it easy to stay centered even if you shift a little. A wider FOV—often marketed as “wide‑angle” or “webcam with wide FOV”—captures more of the surrounding space. Here’s how that plays out in real‑world calls:

| Situation | Narrow FOV (≈60°) | Wide FOV (≈90°) |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| One‑on‑one meeting, static desk | Keeps you centered, minimal distortion | Shows more of your desk, may add slight edge distortion |
| Group call from a small room | Requires you to move the camera or zoom | Fits two‑people side‑by‑side without repositioning |
| Presenting a whiteboard or printed notes | You have to hold the material close to the camera | You can place the board a foot away and stay in frame |
| Working from a cluttered background | Background may dominate the shot | You can crop out distractions by adjusting your position |

A 90‑degree FOV, like the one on the **EMEET C960 1080P Webcam**, strikes a practical balance. It’s wide enough to accommodate occasional movement and small group calls, yet not so extreme that facial features become noticeably stretched.
