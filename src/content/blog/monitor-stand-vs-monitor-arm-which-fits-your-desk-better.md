## Why Choose a Monitor Stand?

A monitor stand is essentially a sturdy platform that lifts your screen to eye level. The WALI Computer Monitor Stand (model STT003) measures **14.6 × 9.3 × 5.5 inches**, giving it a compact footprint that fits comfortably on most desks, even those with limited depth. Its metal construction and rubber pads keep the monitor steady while protecting the desk from scratches.

Because the stand is self‑supporting, you avoid the risk of a clamp slipping or a mounting hole weakening the desk over time. For users who prefer a “set it and forget it” approach, the stand’s simplicity is a big win.

## Why Choose a Monitor Arm?

If you frequently switch between sitting and standing, or you need to share a desk with a partner, a monitor arm offers the flexibility to move the screen out of the way entirely. Arms can swivel 90 degrees or more, tilt for glare reduction, and extend forward to create extra desk space for a keyboard or drawing tablet.

Arms are also a good match for multi‑monitor setups, where each screen can be positioned independently without crowding the desktop.

## Key Factors to Compare

### Desk Space & Layout

- **Stand**: Takes up a small rectangle but still occupies surface area. Works well on desks that already have a built‑in cable management tray or a protective mat. Pair it with a waterproof desk pad to keep the surface clean.  
- **Arm**: Clears the entire desktop, which is a boon for cramped workstations or when you need room for a laptop, notebook, or drawing pad.

### Adjustability & Ergonomics

- **Stand**: Provides a fixed height; you can only tilt the monitor itself. Good enough if you already have a comfortable chair height.  
- **Arm**: Lets you fine‑tune height, tilt, and distance. Ideal for ergonomic setups where the top of the screen should be at or just below eye level.

### Installation & Desk Compatibility

- **Stand**: No drilling, no clamps. Perfect for rented apartments or desks with delicate finishes.  
- **Arm**: Requires a sturdy edge or a pre‑drilled hole. Some desks with thin laminate may not hold a clamp securely.

### Cable Management

- **Stand**: Cables run down the back of the monitor and can be tucked into a desk grommet or a cable‑organizer strip. The anti‑skid pads keep the stand from sliding as you move cables.  
- **Arm**: Many arms include built‑in cable channels that hide wires along the arm, keeping the workspace tidy. If you already use adjustable cord straps, an arm can integrate nicely.

## Who This Isn't For
